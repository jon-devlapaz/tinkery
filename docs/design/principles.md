# Principles

Read [the constitution](../yohaku.md) first; it wins conflicts and only the operator
amends it. These notes explain design application, not a second constitution.

- **Controls:** the quiet key bar shows available actions, not a catalogue. Name
  what the person does, not the underlying protocol.
- **Consent:** layout, copying, answering and skipping grant no approval. Goal
  confirmation, seed confirmation, intake approval and implementation permission
  are separate gates.
- **Status:** missing evidence is unknown, not zero, success or demo data.
- **Keyboard:** keep a keyboard route. Terminal-host shortcuts and OSC 52 clipboard
  forwarding are capabilities to test, not universal promises.

## Visual meaning

Use the constitution's [colour roles](../yohaku.md#colour-has-one-job-each),
including its non-colour cues. Every launch starts on light paper. Ctrl-T, or a click
on the muted `dark`/`light` label beside `?`, switches every screen at once to warm
charcoal (never pure black) and back. Roles keep their jobs and at least 4.5:1 contrast.
The choice lasts until Tinkery exits: nothing reads the system appearance or saves it.
Ctrl-T never types. Without colour the label is hidden and nothing changes.
After an answer, changed goal lines stay in full ink and unchanged lines rest
muted; the distinction clears on typing or sending. No extra banner or model call.

## Why these choices

Dumping before organizing avoids production blocking; structure should emerge
rather than be required at entry. A question about intent is more valuable than
sticky rearrangement for its own sake. Exact evidence and visibly provisional
readings reduce, but cannot eliminate, framing/fixation. Research supports these
principles, not a claim that this terminal beats chat: that needs operator dogfood.
Useful foundations: [Shipman & Marshall on incremental formalization](https://people.engr.tamu.edu/shipman/formality-paper/harmful.html),
[Horvitz on mixed-initiative help](https://www.erichorvitz.com/chi99horvitz.pdf),
and [Shape Up on boundaries](https://basecamp.com/shapeup).
